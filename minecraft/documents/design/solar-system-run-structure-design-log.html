Antithesis Conquering Simulator — Design Log

Solar System Run Structure

Session: Run structure rework — solar systems, time-as-resource, XP/currency split Addendum to: NEW_VISION.md Status: Design exploration — not implemented, not fully numbered
READ ME FIRST — This document is a working synthesis of one design conversation, not a locked spec. It replaces the flat "10 planets, choose 1 of 3 worlds" loop from NEW_VISION.md with a two-tier structure (persistent meta-progression + single-system attempts). Several mechanics are agreed in shape but not in number — see Open Questions before treating anything here as final. Ideas explicitly discarded during this session are logged in Rejected Ideas so they aren't re-proposed without new information.

Starting point

Why the old loop needed rework

NEW_VISION.md's loop — choose 1 of 3 worlds → difficulty → clear/survive → upgrade → next world → boss — reveals everything one screen at a time. Nothing about it was broken exactly, but five gaps in it drove this whole session.

GAP 01

No preview. Each "choose 1 of 3 worlds" screen is rolled fresh when it opens. A player never sees more than the immediate next choice — no way to know what a run holds.

GAP 02

No progress legibility. Nothing shows a player where they are relative to where they're going, run to run.

GAP 03

Thin content at scale. 10 fixed planets total doesn't hold up across repeat runs.

GAP 04

No reason to linger. Clearing a planet's threshold and leaving immediately was always strictly correct — nothing rewarded staying longer.

GAP 05

"The Encounter" problem. Already a known con in NEW_VISION.md — a good run ruined by a poorly-balanced final boss with no structural cushion.

Core structure

The two loops

Everything resolves into a persistent meta-loop (The Ship, between attempts) and a self-contained attempt-loop (one attempt = one solar system). This is the roguelite pattern already named in NEW_VISION.md's pillars — permadeath per attempt, meta-progression across attempts — applied literally, rather than the "one continuous marathon run" alternative that was considered and set aside.

Meta loop — persists across attempts, lives at The Ship

01

Every attempt ends at the Ship — win, death, or timeout all route back here.Currency banked: full amount + an "Efficiency Bonus" on unspent currency for a clean win; partial amount on death or timeout.

02

Currency buys permanent capability, not just stat totals — new accessories entering the pool, new biome types becoming available, a second accessory slot, and the meta-thresholds that let Plant-corrupted systems start appearing.Deliberately not just "numbers go up" — capability unlocks give a stronger reason to keep playing than raw stat inflation.

03

Offered 3 fresh solar system topologies to start the next attempt. Regenerated every time — no fixed roster to memorize or get stuck with.Optional currency-cost reroll if none of the 3 fit what you want to play right now.

04

Offer skew shifts with meta-progress. Early on, offers are mostly clean systems. Deeper into meta-progression, more of the 3 offered are Plant-corrupted (harder).Never forced — normal systems and reroll remain available at any point.

05

Difficulty scaling reuses existing planned systems — EnemyHostility, EnvironmentHostility/TerrainDensity, Gravity, and the modifier framework (Low Gravity, Heavy Fog, Alien Surface) already scoped in the engineering docs. Meta-progression just widens the ranges RunManager rolls from.Clock length is a minor dial here, not a primary one — see Decision 18.

Attempt loop — one attempt = one solar system

01

Pick 1 of 3 solar system topologies. Vague info only — planet count (7–20), rough difficulty spread, distance/time cost between nodes, biome-flavor read, corrupted or not. Commit; no switching, no returning to the ones you passed on.

02

Enter with local fog. Current planet fully known. Next planet known roughly. Everything past that is hidden until you get closer.Chosen specifically to preserve preparation without collapsing all discovery — see Decision 03.

03

Shared clock starts (10 minutes was the original working number — treat as unset, see Open Questions).

04

Per planet: fight until the kill-threshold clears, unlocking the hop — or keep fighting past it. Kills feed XP, which is the actual reason to linger.Hopping likely costs clock time and reveals the next-next planet's rough info.

05

Occasional waystation nodes — visible on the topology, not on every hop — host the shop. Detouring to one costs clock time on purpose: a real routing decision, not a menu tax.

06

Reach the sun, destroy it. That's a clean clear. If the clock hits zero first, you're forced into the sun fight early at a defined disadvantage (mechanics still TBD — see Open Questions).

07

Die, or lose the forced-early sun fight → attempt ends, partial currency banked, back to the Ship.

08

Clear enough Plant-corrupted systems across separate attempts (not within one sitting) → Plant's own system starts appearing as an offer. Clearing that is where THE PLANT fight lives — the actual final boss of the meta-progression, not of any single attempt.

09

Beating THE PLANT is additive, not a reset. Nothing already unlocked is lost. Leans on COSMOS_LORE.md's existing language — "the next run's planets may be slightly different... nothing confirmed, nothing explained" — rather than a new story-unlock screen. Fits "fun over narrative" better than an invented reward beat.

Resources

Currency & XP — two pools, not three

The instinct going in was three currencies (in-run money, meta money, in-run levels). It collapses cleanly into two, because in-run currency and meta-currency were already agreed to be the same pool, Dead Cells–style.

XP — in-run only, resets every attempt
Earned from kills. Spent on accessory picks and upgrades as you level. This is the deliberate answer to "why linger on a planet" — more kills, more XP, a stronger loadout for the next planet. Can be topped off with Currency at a deliberately bad conversion rate, as a smoothing tool for a sparse planet — not a competing strategy to actually fighting.
Currency — earned in-run, dual-purpose
Spendable at waystation shops mid-attempt (health, temporary boosts). Whatever's unspent when the attempt ends converts into the Ship's meta-currency — partial on death/timeout, full plus an Efficiency Bonus on a clean clear. One resource doing both the spend-now and hoard-for-later jobs on purpose, to keep that tension real.

Decision log

Each entry: what was decided, why, and how settled it is. Locked-in = strongly converged on, low ambiguity left. Leaning = the direction chosen, but implementation details still open. Tentative = raised and plausible, not stress-tested yet.

01

Solar systems are procedurally generated per-offer, not a fixed roster

A small fixed set of hand-built systems would mean some are just permanently worse, and repeat plays become memorization. Fresh generation per offer means a bad draw is this attempt's luck, not a standing flaw.

Locked-in
02

Topology preview shows category, not contents

Planet count, difficulty tier, distance/time cost, flavor — never the actual terrain or enemy composition. Keeps local discovery alive even with a map in front of you (the FTL / Slay the Spire pattern, not the Mario-map pattern).

Locked-in
03

Local fog: current planet known, next known roughly, rest hidden

Full visibility kills tension; full mystery makes preparation impossible. Knowing your immediate next stop lets you prepare without collapsing the "I don't fully know what's out there" feeling.

Locked-in
04

One attempt = one solar system, not a chain of systems

A marathon run chaining multiple 7–20-planet systems (plus 3 Plant systems) directly conflicts with the stated "fast paced" goal. Keeping an attempt to a single system, bounded by the clock, is what makes fast and roguelite compatible at all.

Locked-in
05

Meta-progression model (Dead Cells / Hades pattern), not single-marathon-run model

NEW_VISION.md already says roguelite, not roguelike — permadeath plus persistent meta-progression is the more literal reading of the game's own stated pillar, not a compromise on it.

Locked-in
06

Single currency pool serves both in-run spending and meta-banking

Avoids inventing a third resource. Creates a real spend-now-vs-hoard-for-later tension instead of two disconnected economies.

Locked-in
07

XP is tied to kills, not to leaving a planet early

A free level-up (or cash bonus) for hopping immediately directly opposes kill-based XP — same decision point, opposite incentives, and whichever resource is worth more wins outright. Kills-as-XP is the actual answer to "why would anyone linger." The clock already rewards speed on its own; it doesn't need a second explicit payout stacked on top.

Locked-in
08

Per-planet time bonus is a flat threshold, not a continuous scale

A continuously-scaled "faster = more bonus" is mathematically identical to the leave-early bonus already rejected in Decision 07, just relabeled. A flat par-time bonus (Dead Cells Timed Doors model) is a discrete skill check with no race-to-zero, and doesn't re-create the same conflict.

Leaning
09

Accessories split into Unique (finite) and Generic stat (unlimited) tiers

10 unique accessories can't sustain level-ups across a system that may run 20 planets deep — the pool runs dry around planet 10. An unlimited, stackable generic tier (damage%, health%, etc.) keeps every level-up meaningful without requiring dozens more unique accessory designs.

Leaning
10

Shop is occasional, tied to waystation nodes, not every hop

A full shop screen on every single planet transition would invert "all combat, no filler" into a menu simulator with combat breaks. Gating it to specific nodes visible on the topology map turns "detour to shop or push to the sun" into a real map-driven decision instead of a universal interruption.

Locked-in
11

Accessory picks at hops stay fast and lightweight (3-choice, no menu)

Different cadence from the shop on purpose — this happens often and needs to stay a beat, not an interruption. Vampire Survivors' level-up screen, not a browsing menu.

Locked-in
12

Unspent currency at a clean clear converts to an "Efficiency Bonus," win-only

Fixes a real problem — finishing a whole system with nothing to show for it is discouraging. Kept win-only (not on death/timeout) so a full clear reads as clearly better than a partial one. Must stay tuned conservatively — the bigger this bonus, the more it tells players to never use the shop at all, undercutting Decision 10.

Tentative — size unset
13

Death and timeout still bank partial currency, not zero

A failed attempt should still feel like forward motion, not a wipe. Softens the sting of permadeath without weakening in-attempt stakes — you still lose the build and the run.

Locked-in
14

Plant-corrupted systems escalate across separate attempts, not within one

Chaining Plant-system clears inside a single sitting was the rejected marathon model. Paced across attempts instead, it keeps the "I need to progress toward this" motivation intact without violating the fast-paced, single-system-per-attempt structure.

Locked-in
15

Beating THE PLANT is additive (prestige), not a reset

Consistent with every other no-punish choice already made (partial banking on death, etc.) — a reset would be a different psychological contract with the player, introduced without a clear reason to want it.

Leaning
16

THE PLANT's aftermath leans on existing lore, not a new unlock screen

COSMOS_LORE.md already states what happens: planets shift slightly, nothing is confirmed or explained. Reusing that is more consistent with "fun over narrative" than inventing a story-reveal screen would be.

Locked-in
17

Meta-difficulty scaling reuses already-planned systems

EnemyHostility, EnvironmentHostility, TerrainDensity, Gravity, and the modifier framework (Low Gravity / Heavy Fog / Alien Surface) are already scoped in the engineering docs. No new systems needed — just widen the ranges RunManager rolls from as meta-progress deepens.

Leaning
18

Shortened clock time is a minor difficulty dial, not a primary one

Enemy density/hostility/environment scale challenge. A shorter clock mostly scales stress, which fights the "fast paced, fun" pillar past a certain point. Lean on density/hostility/environment to carry most of the difficulty curve.

Tentative

Do not re-propose without new information

Rejected ideas

Each of these was considered directly in this session and set aside for a stated reason. Logged so a future pass doesn't re-litigate the same ground from scratch.

✕
Free level-up or cash bonus for leaving a planet the moment you cross the kill-thresholdDirectly opposes kill-based XP (Decision 07) — rewards both directions of the same choice, so whichever payout is bigger wins and the other stops mattering.
✕
Continuously-scaled "beat expected time by more, get more bonus"Mathematically the same conflict as the leave-early bonus above, just relabeled as a speedrun split. Replaced with a flat threshold (Decision 08).
✕
Marathon model: one run chains multiple full solar systems, including 3 Plant systems, before the final bossToo long and high-dedication per sitting; conflicts with the stated "fast paced progression" goal. Replaced by one-system-per-attempt plus cross-attempt meta-progression (Decisions 04–05, 14).
✕
Fully authored, fixed planet rosters (hand-built, no regeneration)Would collapse replayability once the roster of ~10 planets is memorized. Procedural-per-offer generation preferred, optionally drawing from the curated biome-preset library that already exists.
✕
Full-visibility topology map, no fog at allRemoves all in-run discovery once a system is entered. Local fog (Decision 03) keeps preparation and mystery both alive.
✕
A new story-unlock screen for beating THE PLANTCOSMOS_LORE.md already specifies subtler aftermath — slightly shifted planets, nothing confirmed or explained. Inventing a reveal screen would fight "fun over narrative" rather than serve it.

Punch list

Open questions

Everything below is agreed in direction but not in number, or flagged but not yet answered at all.

Numbers & thresholds still undefined

  • Per-planet kill-threshold to unlock a hop — needs to be low enough that a player having fun clears it naturally, never a farming chore.
  • Clock duration baseline — 10 minutes was the original working number, never validated.
  • "Severe disadvantage" for a forced-early sun fight — needs a concrete mechanic (fewer accessories than a full run would give? a skipped-straight-to-hard boss phase? an environmental hazard tied to proximity to a sun?), not just the adjective.
  • Accessory-upgrade frequency relative to system size — every hop? Every N planets? Scaled to system length?
  • How many Plant-corrupted systems is "enough" to unlock Plant's own system.
  • Reroll cost for rejecting a bad set of 3 system offers.
  • Efficiency Bonus size — must stay modest; oversized and it silently kills the shop's spend-vs-hoard tension (Decision 12).
  • Par-time formula — intended to derive from EnemyDensity and the kill-quota threshold rather than being hand-authored per planet, but the actual derivation isn't specified.
  • Generic-accessory stacking model — additive or multiplicative; produces very different numbers by planet 15+.
  • Accessory pool weighting curve — unique-weighted early (build identity forms fast) vs. generic-weighted late (uniques scarce by then) was suggested, not confirmed.
  • XP-topoff conversion rate — must stay inefficient relative to actual combat XP, or it becomes a competing strategy to fighting rather than a smoothing tool.

Design questions not yet answered

  • Reward legibility. With the Efficiency Bonus, par-time bonus, base kill economy, and XP-topoff spending all live at once, does a player still know why they got what they got in the moment — or does it blur into "numbers happened"?
  • Whether topology mood tags communicate directly (e.g. a visible "short / brutal" vs "long / manageable" read on the offer itself) so choosing among the 3 offers is the input into run shape, rather than relying only on reroll as an escape hatch.
  • Win condition mapping. Exploration / Survival / Combat (NEW_VISION.md) were never explicitly redefined against the clock-and-hop-threshold model — this mapping is still open.

Reference

Glossary

Attempt
One playthrough of a single solar system, bounded by the shared clock. Ends in a win (sun destroyed), a death, or a timeout.
The Ship
Persistent hub between attempts. No combat here — where meta-currency gets spent on permanent unlocks.
Solar System
A procedurally generated topology of 7–20 planets arranged around a sun. The container for one attempt.
Topology Preview
The vague, pre-commitment view of a solar system's shape — planet count, rough difficulty, distance/time costs, flavor tags. Contents stay hidden.
Local Fog
Current planet known in full; next planet known roughly; everything beyond that hidden until reached.
Waystation Node
An occasional non-combat node hosting the shop. Visible on the topology; detouring to it costs clock time.
XP
In-run only resource, earned via kills, spent on accessory picks and upgrades. Resets every attempt.
Currency
Earned in-run, spendable at waystations, converts to meta-currency at attempt end — partial on death/timeout, full plus Efficiency Bonus on a clean win.
Unique Accessory
One of the ~10 named, build-defining accessories (Super Jump, Little Friend, etc.). Finite pool.
Generic / Stat Accessory
Percentage-based stat boosts (damage, health, etc.). Unlimited, stackable pool — solves late-system pool exhaustion.
Plant-Corrupted System
A harder-difficulty solar system variant that appears with increasing frequency as meta-progress deepens.
Plant's System / THE PLANT
The meta-progression's final unlock and final boss, gated behind clearing enough Plant-corrupted systems across separate attempts.
Sun
The per-attempt capstone objective. Destroying it clears the solar system.
